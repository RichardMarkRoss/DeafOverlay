HUD concepts

Three look-and-feel directions for the Heroes Without Sound overlay: the in-game HUD plus its settings. The sounds are simulated, but they follow the real DSP's output: direction, loudness, and front/behind (real on 5.1/7.1, guessed on stereo). Try the tweaks. They apply to all three concepts at once.

Orbit

An evolution of today's sphere and ring: lighter, smaller, and readable. Conservative
Window 240 × 240 px, below the crosshair
  • Keeps the brand's 3D identity, but the dotted sphere becomes a faint wireframe, so it stops competing with the game's white HUD.
  • F/R/B/L ticks, plus a chevron shape for front/behind. Direction never relies on colour.
  • 40% of today's footprint, with a dark scrim so it stays readable on bright walls.

02

Radar

A flat top-down disc: you're in the middle, and sectors light up toward sounds. Middle ground
Window 200 × 200 px, lower centre
  • The easiest mental model: top-down like a minimap, with 16 sectors and numbered bearings.
  • Wedge length is loudness, with rings as the scale. Behind-you wedges are hatched, not just recoloured.
  • Crosshair mode shrinks it to thin arcs around the aim point, so your eyes never leave the action.

03

Compass tape

A thin 360° bearing strip under the game's top HUD. Nothing covers the action. Novel
Window 520 × 72 px, top centre
  • Front at the centre, BEHIND at both ends. Shooter players already read compass tapes, so it's a familiar pattern.
  • On stereo the rear of the tape is greyed out and labelled. It says plainly what stereo can't know, instead of hiding it.
  • The smallest footprint of the three, and it sits where the eye already checks the score and timer.
